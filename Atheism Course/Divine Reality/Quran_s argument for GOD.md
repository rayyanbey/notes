##### Introduction:
- waking up in a sealed stone room to find a desk and computer suddenly appear, with a message claiming they came from “nothing.”
- The natural human intuition and reasoning reject the idea that something can come from nothing or cause itself, pointing instead to a prior cause as the most rational explanation.
- This basic reasoning is reflected in the Qur’an’s argument about the origin of finite things, which states four possibilities:

1. Came from nothing
    
2. Created itself
    
3. Created by something else created
    
4. Created by something uncreated

- The Qur’an actively engages readers by using rational, intellectual arguments and posing deep questions, indicating it values human reasoning.
    
- Scholar Rosalind Ward Gwynne observes that the Qur’an’s use of reason shaped Islamic scholarship and emphasized the need for humans to base actions on reasons.
    
- Early scholars, including Ibn Taymiyya, affirmed that rational thinking supports revelation, and both are complementary tools in understanding and proving the truth of Islam.


#### Quranic Argument: #QuranicArgumentAgainstAtheism
- ***“Or were they created by nothing? Or were they the creators [of themselves]? Or did they create the heavens and Earth? Rather, they are not certain.”***  Surah tur
- This argument can also be turned into a universal formula that does not require reference to scripture:
1. The universe is finite.
2. Finite things could have come from nothing, created themselves, been ultimately created by something created, or been created by something uncreated.
3. They could not have come from nothing, created themselves, or have been ultimately created by something created.
4. Therefore, they were created by something uncreated.


#### Universe is finite:
This passage argues that the physical universe cannot be actually infinite because an actual, differentiated infinite leads to logical contradictions when applied to reality. Through thought experiments—such as a bag with infinite balls or a stack of endlessly smaller cubes—the author demonstrates how applying infinity to the real world produces absurd outcomes, suggesting that infinity can only exist as a conceptual or mathematical idea, not as a physical reality. Since the universe is composed of differentiated parts (like atoms, stars, events), it cannot be actually infinite and must therefore be finite and have a beginning. While scientific theories remain inconclusive due to underdetermination, this philosophical reasoning supports the conclusion that the universe had a finite starting point. The passage prepares to explore the possible causes of this beginning in the next section.  (AYE MEINU SAMJH NAHI AYI)

##### created from nothing:
- **Definition of Nothing**: "Nothing" is the complete absence of everything—no matter, energy, potential, or causal conditions.  What is 0 + 0 + 0? It is not 3, it’s 0.
    
- **Common Misunderstanding**: The quantum vacuum is often mistaken for "nothing," but it is actually a physical entity governed by laws of physics.
    
- **Philosophical Principle**: Something cannot come from nothing; being cannot arise from nonbeing. This idea is grounded in rational and metaphysical reasoning.
    
- **Logical Consequence**: If something could come from nothing, then anything—including the universe—could randomly appear or disappear without cause, which is irrational.
    
- **Quantum Argument Refuted**: The emergence of particles from the quantum vacuum does not prove creation from nothing, because the quantum vacuum itself is a real, physical field with properties.

#### How did God create from nothing?
- The claim that God created the universe from nothing does not imply "something from nothing" in an absolute sense because God Himself is the causal agent through His will and power.
    
- "Nothing" means non-being—no potential, no causal power—so something cannot emerge from true "nothingness" without a cause.
    
- In Islamic thought, creation from nothing refers to the absence of pre-existing material, not the absence of causal conditions—God’s will and power are those conditions.
    
- The idea that the universe created itself is logically incoherent; it would require the universe to exist before it existed, which is a contradiction.
    
- A created thing is, by definition, finite and once non-existent, so it could not have had the power to bring itself into being.
    
- A comparison is made: just as your mother cannot give birth to herself, the universe cannot create itself.
    
- Al-Khattabi argues that non-existent things cannot have power or perform actions, further invalidating self-creation.
    
- An objection based on asexual reproduction (e.g., in single-celled organisms) is false because reproduction requires a pre-existing parent, and thus doesn't support self-creation.
    
- The inclusion of flawed atheist objections demonstrates the irrationality and desperation of some counter-arguments against God's existence.
