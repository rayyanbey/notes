## Abu Bakr's (RA) Instructions to the Muslim Army — Summary in Points

Before the army departed, Abu Bakr (RA) gave the soldiers **ten important instructions** that established the Islamic ethics of warfare.

### His Ten Instructions

1. **Do not betray trusts or agreements.**
    - Remain faithful to treaties and promises.
2. **Do not steal from the spoils of war (Ghulul).**
    - War booty must be distributed lawfully.
3. **Do not deceive or act dishonestly.**
    - Muslims must maintain integrity even during war.
4. **Do not mutilate the bodies of enemy soldiers.**
    - Respect human dignity, even after death.
5. **Do not cut down fruit-bearing trees.**
    - Protect agriculture and the environment unless absolutely necessary.
6. **Do not slaughter livestock unnecessarily.**
    - Sheep, cows, and camels should only be killed for food, not for destruction.
7. **Do not harm monks or people devoted to worship in monasteries.**
    - Leave religious people who are not fighting alone and respect their places of worship.
8. **Remember Allah before eating food offered to you.**
    - If people offer meals containing different foods, begin by mentioning Allah's name (saying _Bismillah_).
9. **Fight only those who are actively engaged in war.**
    - Abu Bakr (RA) described enemy combatants by their appearance and instructed the soldiers to direct their fighting against them, not against non-combatants.
10. **March forward in the name of Allah.**

- The army's mission should be carried out sincerely for Allah and according to His guidance.

## Speech Must be Accompanied by Action & the Role of Youth in Serving Islam — Summary in Points

### Appointment of Usamah (RA)

- Usamah ibn Zayd was appointed commander of the Muslim army at only **18–20 years of age**.
- Many senior Companions found this difficult because they were older, more experienced, and included veterans of major battles like Badr and Uhud.
- However, since the Prophet ﷺ had appointed him, they were obliged to accept and obey his leadership.

### Abu Bakr (RA) Led by Example

Rather than merely telling others to respect Usamah (RA), Abu Bakr (RA) demonstrated it through his actions:

#### 1. Honoring Usamah (RA)

- Abu Bakr (RA), although the Caliph and over 60 years old, **walked beside Usamah's mounted horse** instead of riding.
- He refused Usamah's request to ride or for him to dismount.
- This publicly affirmed Usamah's authority and taught the army to respect its appointed leader.

#### 2. Seeking Permission

- Abu Bakr (RA) wanted Umar (RA) to remain in Madinah as his advisor.
- Instead of ordering it, he **asked Usamah's permission**, recognizing him as the army's commander.
- This reinforced that leadership should be respected regardless of age.

### Speech Must Match Actions

- Abu Bakr (RA) practiced what he preached.
- He understood that leaders must lead by example, not merely by words.
- This reflects the Qur'anic teaching:
    - Do not command others to do good while neglecting it yourself (Qur'an 2:44).

### The Role of Young People in Islam
#youth_empowerment_by_ProphetSAW
- The Prophet ﷺ entrusted a young man with leading an expedition against the world's greatest superpower.
- Usamah (RA) successfully completed the mission and returned victorious.
- His example shows that **ability, faith, and character—not age alone—qualify a person for leadership**.
- Throughout Islamic history, young Muslims played vital roles in establishing and strengthening the Muslim community.

## Key Lessons

- Leadership should be based on merit and trustworthiness, not age.
- Respect lawful leadership, even when the leader is younger.
- Effective leaders demonstrate their principles through actions.
- Islam empowers capable youth to take significant responsibilities in serving the religion and society.
