### No ultimate purpose #NoUltimatePurpose
- Philosophers like **Wittgenstein** and **Camus** admit they don't know the purpose of life. Wittgenstein believed life isn’t just for pleasure, while Camus suggested we should stop searching for meaning and just live in a way that works for us. 
- However, this view is challenged by the argument that even simple objects like chairs and clothes have a clear purpose—so how can humans, the most complex beings, have no purpose? It seems unnatural to think that way. 
- The **naturalist** (atheist) view says we’re the result of blind, random physical processes. But this means we’re just riding a sinking ship—the universe will eventually die, and humanity with it. If life ends in nothing, then what’s the point of anything we do? 
- Some atheists respond by saying we can make our own purpose. But the problem with this is that it’s pretending—we’re giving ourselves made-up meaning while knowing there’s no real foundation behind it. It’s like children playing pretend; it’s not real.
- Others, like **Richard Dawkins**, say our purpose is to pass on our genes. But this turns human life into a meaningless biological accident—just a product of molecular processes.
- In contrast, **Islam** offers a clear, intuitive, and empowering answer: life is a test, and our purpose is to know and worship God. Our actions have eternal consequences, and we are not just meaningless matter—we are conscious, moral beings created for a higher purpose.
