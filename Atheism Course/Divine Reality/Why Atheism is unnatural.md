#### Self evident truths: #selfEvident
- Some beliefs are **self-evidently true**, meaning they are accepted by default and don't require external proof (e.g., causality, the past, reasoning, other minds).

- When such truths are questioned, the burden of proof lies with the skeptic—not the believer.

- Self-evident truths are **universal**, **untaught**, **natural**, and **intuitive**—arising from human nature, not culture or instruction.

- The **reality of the past** is an example: nearly all people and cultures accept it instinctively, without needing to be taught.

- Denying the past (or other self-evident truths) creates more confusion than clarity and fails to explain key aspects of human experience.

### Psychological Evidence:
- Contemporary research in developmental psychology and cognitive science suggests that **belief in God or a purposeful creator may be natural and intuitive**, especially in children. Studies by scholars like Deborah Kelemen and Olivera Petrovich indicate that children across cultures tend to attribute **purpose and design** to natural phenomena (a tendency known as **teleological reasoning**). Even when raised without religious input, children intuitively believe that **objects and events are designed for a purpose**, often associating them with the actions of a creator.

- Adults, even those identifying as atheists or agnostics, can revert to this intuitive mode of thinking under **cognitive strain or time pressure**, showing that **teleological thinking persists** into adulthood. In contrast, atheism often appears to require **cognitive effort and cultural reinforcement**, making it more of an **acquired worldview** than a natural one. Some researchers suggest that suppressing belief in God may involve **self-regulation**, as atheism counters many innate tendencies of the human mind.

- While the research does not conclusively prove God's existence, it highlights that **belief in a creator aligns with our psychological defaults**, whereas atheism may go against the grain of how human cognition is wired. The findings challenge the notion that religion is merely a product of indoctrination, suggesting instead that **theism could be part of our cognitive architecture**.

### Sociological anthropological
- Professor **Justin Barrett**, in his book _Born Believers_, presents evidence that **children are naturally inclined to believe in a divine creator**—a concept he refers to as **"natural religion."** His research in developmental psychology shows that children instinctively perceive the world as created by a **supernatural, purposeful being**, not by humans or natural forces. ***HADITH OF PROPHET SAW REGARDING FITRAH***

- From a very young age—even as early as the **first year of life**—children can differentiate between **agents (with intentions)** and **non-agents (objects or forces)**. They are especially attuned to **detecting agency** and often infer that **order and function in nature** are results of intentional creation. This leads them to conclude that the world was made by a **minded, divine being**, not random chance or impersonal forces.

- Barrett concludes that children are essentially **"born believers,"** naturally predisposed to believe in God without being explicitly taught. This supports the view that **theism is embedded in our cognitive framework**, while atheism may require cultural or intellectual effort to develop.

### Is atheism the default position? #atheismDefault
- Some atheists claim that **atheism is the default or self-evident truth**, requiring no justification. However, this claim is challenged by the argument that **belief in a creator is actually more intuitive and natural**, while atheism is something that must be **learned and adopted** through reasoning or exposure.

- The **rejection of a creator or cause is not self-evident**—rather, it's a **counter-intuitive** stance that raises more questions than it answers. For example:

- **How did the universe come from nothing?**
    
- **Why does a contingent universe exist at all without a cause?**
    

- While atheists may offer alternative explanations for the universe’s existence, these explanations are not **obvious** or **default**—they are **acquired views** that require supporting evidence.

- The key point is this: If belief in a creator is the more **natural and basic position**, then the burden of proof lies on those who **reject it**. Instead of asking for proof of God, the more rational approach is to ask:  
- **What compelling reason or evidence is there to deny a creator?**

### Fitrah  #fitrah
- **Fitrah** refers to the **natural state or innate disposition** that God has placed within every human being. It includes an **inborn knowledge of God** and a natural inclination to **worship Him**.
- According to a well-known **Prophetic tradition**, every child is born in this state, but **external influences** (such as culture, parents, and society) can cloud or distort it. #hadith 
- Classical scholars like **Al-Ghazali** and **Ibn Taymiyya** affirmed that:
                 - The fitrah contains a **deep, intuitive awareness of God**.
    
                - While rational arguments can help, they are mainly **reminders** rather than sources of new knowledge.
    
             - If the fitrah is **obscured**, people may need **additional evidences** to be guided.

- In **Islamic epistemology**, **belief in God is not based solely on rational or empirical evidence**, but on uncovering this inner, intuitive knowledge through reflection, spiritual awakening, and God's will.
- Rational arguments serve as **tools** to awaken this latent belief, much like rediscovering a forgotten toy from childhood—something we already knew but were reminded of.
- The Qur’an encourages people to **reflect deeply**, using verses like:

- “Or were they created by nothing?” (52:35)
    
- “Can there be doubt about God, Creator of the heavens and Earth?” (14:10)
- Ultimately, **guidance comes from God**. Rational proofs may lead someone to truth, but only God can **open a person’s heart** to accept it.
- Islamic scholar **Wesam Charkawi** affirms that the **sense of God’s existence** is as natural and deeply felt as emotions like love, pain, or joy—**undeniable even without external proof**.
