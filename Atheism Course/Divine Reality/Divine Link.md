### Plausibility of existence and non existence at the same time?
- Some atheists argue that the universe, like God, could be considered independent and necessary.
    
- This argument is flawed because the universe is not necessary—it could have not existed.
    
- The universe’s components (e.g., quarks or quantum fields) could be arranged differently, which indicates contingency and dependency.
    
- All observable things in the universe have limited, specific physical qualities like size and shape.
    
- Since everything within the universe is finite and dependent, the universe as a whole cannot be considered independent or necessary.

#### Dimagh band strategy:
- Some argue the universe is a "brute fact" that simply exists without explanation, as Bertrand Russell claimed.
    
- This view avoids the intellectual responsibility of seeking an explanation for the universe.
    
- The “hovering green ball” analogy shows that we naturally seek explanations for things, no matter their size.
    
- Claiming the universe is a brute fact contradicts the scientific method, which seeks to explain phenomena.
    
- Cosmology, a branch of science, is dedicated to understanding the universe’s origin and nature supporting the importance of such inquiry.

#### Chachu Science labb len ge jawab:
- The objection that "science will eventually find an answer" is based on misunderstanding the argument from dependency.
    
- This argument is not scientific but metaphysical—it addresses the nature of existence and dependency, not empirical phenomena.
    
- Even scientific theories like multiple universes still involve dependent entities that require explanation.
    
- Science only works with empirical and observable (i.e., dependent) things, so it cannot discover something truly independent and eternal.
    
- Since science is confined to the study of dependent things, it cannot provide an ultimate explanation for the existence of the universe.


##### Dependent objects create independent object? 🤷‍♂️
The objection based on the fallacy of composition argues that just because individual parts of the universe are dependent does not mean the whole universe is also dependent. However, this objection is flawed. In reality, dependent parts generally form dependent wholes—as seen in a house made of dependent materials. Likewise, the universe, composed of dependent elements with limited qualities, must also be dependent. The burden of proof lies on the critic to show that dependent parts can form an independent whole.

**Key Points:**
  
- The fallacy of composition assumes the whole has the same qualities as its parts, which is not always wrong.
    
- Dependent parts usually form a dependent whole.
    
- A house made of dependent materials is also dependent.
    
- The universe, made of dependent things, is itself dependent.
    
- Critics must prove that dependent parts can form an independent whole.
