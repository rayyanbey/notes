#westerHistory 
- **Antiquity and Early Mentions:**
    
    - Atheism was not a widespread movement in antiquity.
        
    - Only a few individuals and philosophers speculated about the gods' origins or rejected religious practices.
        
    - The term "atheism" was first used by Sir John Cheke in a translation of Plutarch’s _On Superstition_.
-  **1600s - France:**
    
    - Atheism sparked polemical writings and socio-political opposition.
        
    - It was perceived as a threat to religious and societal norms.
        
- **1700s - Britain:**
    
    - Joseph Addison’s _The Evidence of the Christian Religion_ contained critiques of atheism.
        
    - Addison described atheists as contradictory and zealots in their disbelief.
        
    - Despite his biased view, the intellectual discourse on atheism grew.
        
    - Thinkers like Kazimierz Lyszczynski (1689) and Matthias Knutzen (1674) openly denied God’s existence.
        
    - David Hume and Voltaire provided philosophical arguments questioning theism and promoting skepticism.
        
    - Voltaire advocated deism, rejecting religious revelation while acknowledging a creator.
        
    - Hume challenged the argument from design, critiqued religious belief in miracles, and raised the problem of evil.
-  **19th Century - Atheism Gains Ground:**
    
    - Charles Bradlaugh, a British MP, fought for societal acceptance of atheism.
        
    - Authored _Humanity’s Gain from Unbelief_, _A Plea for Atheism_, and _Doubts in Dialogue_.
        
    - His writings sought to remove prejudices against atheists and argued that skepticism benefited humanity.
        
-  **1920s - Rise of Logical Positivism:**
    
    - This movement, influenced by scientific progress, demanded empirical verification for meaningful statements.
        
    - Anything beyond sensory perception (e.g., God, morality, metaphysics) was considered meaningless.
        
    - Atheism became the default position as God could not be empirically verified.
        
-  **Post-1960s - Decline of Logical Positivism:**
    
    - The movement was self-defeating: its verification principle could not be empirically verified.
        
    - This led to its downfall and allowed for the intellectual resurgence of theism.
-  **20th Century - Theism’s Comeback:**
    
    - _Time_ magazine (1980) noted a resurgence of theism among academic philosophers.
        
    - Scientific discoveries contributed to this shift:
        
        - The _Big Bang_ suggested a cosmic beginning, challenging the notion of an eternal universe.
            
        - Fine-tuning in physics suggested an arrangement conducive to conscious life.
            
        - Molecular biology advancements, such as the discovery of DNA’s structure, hinted at complexity beyond chance.
            
    - Even atheist Francis Crick speculated about an external influence in the origins of genetic information.
