#### baiah 
- The term _Bai'ah_ is often translated as "pledge of allegiance" out of necessity, as there is no single English word that captures its full nuances, which are exclusive to Islam. #baiah 
- Ibn Khuldoon defined _Bai'ah_ as "the covenant one takes to obey the one who has authority (over the people)". #ibnKhaldunOnBaiah 
- Others define it as a "pledge to apply the teachings of Islam". #DefOfBaiah 
- Based on the nomination process of Abu Bakr, when qualified leaders are chosen by people of knowledge and wisdom, the Muslim masses are encouraged to unite around them to preserve unity and stand firmly against internal and external enemies. #SelectingRuler 
- ![[Pasted image 20260709123004.png]] Understanding the concept of baiah  and the current implementation #baiahQuestion
- **Bai'ah** is a pledge of **loyalty and obedience** to the **Khaleefah (Caliph)** of the Muslim Ummah.
- The Khaleefah, in return, is obligated to:
- Rule according to the **Qur'an and Sunnah**.
- Implement **Islamic law (Shari'ah)** in beliefs, legislation, and governance.
- - Muslims are obligated to **obey the Khaleefah** as long as he does **not command disobedience to Allah**.
- The Prophet ﷺ warned against **rebellion or competing leadership**, as it causes:
    - Division of the Ummah.
    - Instability.
    - Bloodshed and civil conflict. #rebellionagainsIslamicGov 
- During the caliphate of Abu Bakr:
    - The people of Madinah gave Bai'ah directly to him.
    - Other regions pledged through his appointed governors.
- Scholars differ regarding **who must give Bai'ah**:
    - **Opinion 1:** Community leaders and people of knowledge pledge directly; the general public follows through their representatives.
    - **Opinion 2:** Every Muslim should personally give Bai'ah to the Khaleefah, citing the example of the general pledge to Abu Bakr.
- The passage argues that **Bai'ah in this political sense** should **only be given to the overall ruler (Khaleefah)** of the Muslim Ummah, not to other individuals or organizations.
- Bai'ah is described as a **contract**:
    - **The Khaleefah promises** to govern by Islam.
    - **The Muslims promise** to obey him within the limits of Islam.
- Bai'ah is presented as a concept **unique to the Islamic system of governance**, not found in other political systems.
- The passage concludes by citing **Qur'an 4:65**, stating that true faith requires accepting the judgments of the Prophet ﷺ without resistance and with complete submission.

#### Accountability   #accountabilityOftheRuler 
- Abu Bakr said:
    
    > "If I do well, then obey me. And if I act wrongly, then correct me."
    
    - This establishes that rulers are **accountable** to the Muslim community.
- Unlike the Prophet ﷺ, later rulers are **not infallible** because:
    - Revelation ended with the Prophet ﷺ.
    - They can make mistakes and must follow the **Qur'an and Sunnah**.
- Citizens have both the **right and responsibility** to:
    - Advise their leaders.
    - Point out their mistakes.
    - Hold them accountable.
    - Encourage them to do what is right according to Islam.
- The Prophet ﷺ taught that **honoring a just ruler** is an act of honoring Allah.
- The hadith **"Religion is sincerity (nasihah)"** includes sincerity towards the **leaders (Imams) of the Muslims**, which means:
    - Helping them fulfill their responsibilities.
    - Uniting under their leadership.
    - Advising them sincerely.
    - Correcting their errors respectfully and honorably.
- Citizens should support their leader when:
    - He governs according to Islam.
    - He calls upon them to defend the Muslim community.
    - He deserves support through just leadership.
- The Companions of the Prophet ﷺ practiced this by:
    - Advising Abu Bakr.
    - Providing him with important information.
    - Assisting him in governing the state.
- The passage argues that many modern Muslim societies have lost this principle because:
    - Citizens fear speaking to or criticizing rulers.
    - Dictatorship and tyranny replace mutual consultation and accountability.
- A successful Islamic government requires a **mutual contract**:
    - **The ruler** governs according to the Qur'an and Sunnah.
    - **The citizens** obey and support him in what is right, while respectfully correcting him when he errs.
- According to the passage, **stability and success** are achieved when both rulers and citizens fulfill these reciprocal responsibilities.

### Taking Control of Affairs on the Domestic Front

- Abu Bakr (RA) understood that governing the Muslim state required delegation and consultation.
- He appointed capable Companions to key positions:
    - Abu Ubaidah ibn al-Jarrah as head of the Muslim Treasury (Bayt al-Mal).
    - Umar ibn al-Khattab as judge (Qadi), while Abu Bakr also personally judged important cases.
    - Zayd ibn Thabit as chief secretary and official scribe.
    - Ali ibn Abi Talib and Uthman ibn Affan also wrote official letters when needed.

### His Livelihood After Becoming Caliph

- Abu Bakr (RA) initially continued his business as a cloth merchant.
- He was seen going to the marketplace carrying garments to sell.
- Umar (RA) and Abu Ubaidah (RA) reminded him that governing the Muslims required his full attention.
- Abu Bakr asked how he would provide for his family's needs if he stopped working.
- A modest stipend was arranged for him from the public treasury.

### His Stipend

- Another narration states he received:
    - **250 dinars annually**, and
    - Certain portions of a sheep.
- Even this amount was insufficient for his family's expenses.
- He sometimes returned to business because the stipend did not fully support his household.
- Umar (RA) found him working while people were waiting for the Caliph to settle disputes, demonstrating the need for him to devote himself completely to public service.

## Abu Bakr (RA): The Ideal Member of Society

### Dedication to Public Service

- He valued every moment of his life.
- Constantly:
    - Educated people.
    - Encouraged good.
    - Forbade evil.
    - Worked for the welfare of society.
- Despite enormous responsibilities, he continued performing small acts of kindness.

### Milking His Neighbours' Goats

- Before becoming Caliph, he regularly milked the goats of women in his neighborhood.
- After becoming Caliph, people assumed he would stop.
- Abu Bakr replied:
    
    > "I will indeed continue to milk them for you. I hope my new position will not change me."
    
- He continued this service for about six months until he moved to central Madinah.

### Lessons from This Incident

- Leadership should not make a person arrogant.
- True leaders continue serving ordinary people.
- Even while leading a growing state and military campaigns, Abu Bakr (RA) remained humble and helpful.

### His Humility

- If his camel's bridle fell, he would dismount and pick it up himself.
- When someone offered to do it for him, he replied that the Prophet ﷺ had instructed them **not to ask people for unnecessary help**.
- He believed humility was a path to Allah's pleasure and honor.

### Prophetic Teaching on Humility

The Prophet ﷺ taught:

- Charity never decreases wealth.
- Forgiving others increases one's honor.
- Whoever humbles himself for Allah, Allah raises his status.

### Secretly Helping an Elderly Blind Woman

- Umar (RA) regularly cared for an old blind woman living outside Madinah.
- Each time he arrived, someone had already cleaned her house and helped her.
- Curious, Umar hid nearby one day.
- He discovered that the mysterious helper was none other than **Abu Bakr (RA)**, despite being the Caliph.

### Visiting Umm Ayman (RA)

- After the Prophet ﷺ passed away, Abu Bakr invited Umar to visit Umm Ayman, just as the Prophet ﷺ used to.
- They found her crying.
- She explained that she was **not crying because of the Prophet's death**, but because **revelation from Allah had ceased**.
- Her words deeply moved Abu Bakr and Umar, and both began to cry with her.

## Key Lessons

- Effective leadership requires delegation and consultation.
- Public office is a trust, not a privilege.
- Leaders deserve only reasonable compensation from public funds.
- Humility should increase with authority, not decrease.
- Great leaders continue serving ordinary people personally.
- Small acts of kindness are as valuable as major achievements.
- Following the Prophet ﷺ in both leadership and character was Abu Bakr's greatest priority.

## Enjoining Good and Forbidding Evil — Summary in Points

### Responsibility of Every Muslim

- The Prophet ﷺ taught that every person is a **shepherd responsible for those under their care**.
- A leader's greatest responsibility is not only material welfare but also the **spiritual guidance** of the people.
- As Caliph, Abu Bakr (RA) viewed guiding the entire Muslim nation toward righteousness as one of his primary duties.

### Correct Understanding of Qur'an 5:105

- Some Muslims wrongly believe they only need to improve themselves.
- Abu Bakr (RA) explained that this verse **does not remove the obligation** of enjoining good and forbidding evil.
- A believer must:
    - Follow the right path.
    - Encourage others to do good.
    - Prevent wrongdoing as much as possible.
- If people reject the advice after it has been conveyed, the believer is **not accountable** for their sins.

### Warning Against Remaining Silent

- Abu Bakr (RA) quoted the Prophet ﷺ:
    - If people witness evil and do not stop it, Allah may punish the entire society.
    - If a wrongdoer is allowed to continue without being restrained, collective punishment can follow.
- This shows that remaining silent in the face of public wrongdoing is dangerous.

### Educating Through Character

Abu Bakr (RA) taught people in several ways:

- By speaking and advising.
- By setting a personal example.
- Sometimes by intentionally **not performing certain recommended (Sunnah) acts** publicly so that people would not mistake them as obligatory.

### Example During Hajj

- Abu Bakr (RA) and Umar (RA) sometimes refrained from sacrificing animals during Hajj.
- Their purpose was to prevent people from believing that the sacrifice was compulsory.

### Respect for Parents

- Abu Bakr (RA) deeply respected his father.
- Whenever he met him, he would quickly dismount from his camel and walk to him as a sign of honor and obedience.

### Advice to His Son

- He advised his son, Abdur-Rahman (RA), not to insult his neighbor during an argument.
- He reminded him that:
    - People's lives pass away.
    - Deeds remain and will be judged on the Day of Resurrection.

### Excellence in Prayer

- Prayer remained central in Abu Bakr's life.
- He performed Salah with complete concentration and excellence.
- Later generations regarded his manner of prayer as an example to emulate.

### Consoling Those in Grief

- When someone lost a loved one, Abu Bakr (RA):
    - Comforted them.
    - Reminded them of the Hereafter.
    - Encouraged patience.
    - Reminded them that even the Prophet ﷺ passed away, helping them bear their own loss.

## His Sermons and Public Advice

### Style of His Sermons

- His sermons were:
    - Brief.
    - Powerful.
    - Rich in wisdom.
    - Focused on practical guidance.

### The Five Darknesses and Five Lamps

Abu Bakr (RA) taught:

|Darkness|Lamp (Solution)|
|---|---|
|Love of the world|Taqwa (God-consciousness)|
|Sin|Repentance|
|Grave|Shahadah (belief in Allah and His Messenger ﷺ)|
|Hereafter|Good deeds|
|Bridge (Sirat)|Certainty of faith (Yaqeen)|

### Advice After the Prophet's ﷺ Death

One year after the Prophet ﷺ passed away, Abu Bakr (RA):

- Became emotional and cried while remembering him.
- Encouraged people to:
    - Ask Allah for well-being.
    - Hold firmly to truthfulness.
    - Avoid lying.
    - Maintain family ties.
    - Avoid jealousy, hatred, and plotting against one another.
    - Live as brothers for the sake of Allah.

### Teaching Modesty

- Abu Bakr (RA) urged Muslims to be shy before Allah.
- He even covered himself carefully while relieving himself in the desert out of modesty before Allah.

### Final Sermon's Main Advice

He reminded Muslims to:

- Fear Allah (Taqwa).
- Praise Allah sincerely.
- Balance hope and fear in worship.
- Perform righteous deeds quickly.
- Hold firmly to the Qur'an.
- Remember that angels record every action.
- Realize that death can come at any time.
- Spend wealth in Allah's cause.
- Avoid wasting life on worldly distractions.
- Remember previous nations, kings, and powerful rulers who all eventually died.
- Understand that no one has a special relationship with Allah except through obedience.
- Seek Allah's forgiveness continually.

## Key Lessons

- Every Muslim shares responsibility for promoting good and preventing evil.
- Silence in the face of wrongdoing can lead to collective consequences.
- Leadership is demonstrated through personal example as much as words.
- Respect for parents, kindness to neighbors, and excellent worship are marks of righteous character.
- Constant remembrance of death and the Hereafter encourages sincere worship and good deeds.
- True success comes through obedience to Allah, not status, wealth, or worldly power.
