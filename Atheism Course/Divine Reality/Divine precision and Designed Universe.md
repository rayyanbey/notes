#####  Electromagnetic Force
- The passage is about the **electromagnetic force**—one of the four fundamental forces of nature.

- It’s responsible for **holding electrons around nuclei**, giving atoms their structure.
    
- Because of it, matter has **strength, shape, and hardness**. Without it, **atoms wouldn’t exist**, and therefore life wouldn’t either.
    
- It enables **chemical bonding**, which is essential for molecules and life processes.
    

Professor John Leslie highlights a fascinating aspect:

- This force has **one specific strength**, but that same strength makes many different processes possible:
    
    - Stars can burn for billions of years.
        
    - Carbon (essential for life) can be formed inside stars.
        
    - Atoms remain stable because leptons don’t replace quarks.
        
    - Protons don’t decay too fast or repel each other excessively, allowing chemistry to exist.
        

This raises the question: how can **one single value** of the electromagnetic force meet all these different requirements? Leslie suggests that the force seems **precisely calibrated (fine-tuned)** to make life and the universe possible.
